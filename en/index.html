RLR Kashiwanoha researches and develops AI robot control for production lines. We address operations that require people to judge and adjust to changes in part position, shape and contact. Our main focus is operations that are especially hard to automate, such as cable routing and assembly.We aim to automate such manual operations by combining learning from example motions, adapting motion to the situation and coordinating multiple operations.Alongside design, development, training and motion checks in simulation, we provide equipment configurations, measurement/control-interface specifications and test plans for physical validation.

Technical challenges in
automating manual operations

Manual assembly and handling may require changes in grasping and motion as part position, orientation, dimensions or deformation vary. Automation requires translating judgments made through sight and touch into information a robot can observe and conditions its controller can use.

For example, inserting a part requires adjusting motion to alignment and contact. Flexible parts change shape even when lifted. Object localization, contact-responsive motion and appropriate support must work together.

A sequence of operations also requires decisions about whether the previous task is complete and the next can begin. Robots, sensors, fixtures and peripheral equipment must exchange information, with defined stopping and recovery procedures for unexpected states.

AI technologies for
automating manual operations

A robot observes the state of the workpiece, performs a motion, checks the result and proceeds to the next operation. Here, a task is divided into units of motion with defined start and end conditions (skills). The following sections introduce two technologies that support this process: skill learning, which combines imitation learning and reinforcement learning to determine the motion within a skill, and skill orchestration, which selects and connects skills.

01 / Skill learning

Skill learningAdd a state-dependent correction to the imitation policy's output

Skill learning determines motion commands within a selected skill. GQARL, which we are developing, is one such proposed learning architecture: imitation learning provides the baseline, and reinforcement learning learns bounded corrections for the current state. The detail page illustrates how they work together and distinguishes the design from the implemented research configuration.

Imitation

Imitation learningLearn how to move from example motions

Pairs of states and the actions taken in them (reference actions) are used as training data to learn a policy that outputs an action for an input state. The initial motion in current sequential-motion tests uses a policy learned from saved records (the imitation policy).

Read the explanation →
Reinforcement

Reinforcement learningLearn corrections from motion results

The output of the imitation policy is used as the base motion. A policy is learned that outputs a correction added to the base motion so that a score of the motion outcome (the reward) increases. The correction is bounded (residual reinforcement learning). Its benefit remains unconfirmed, and it is not used in current sequential-motion tests.

02 / Skill orchestration

Skill orchestrationPredict the result and select the next operation

Skill orchestration predicts the state after a motion, selects the next skill, and handles connections between skills and recovery from failures. WMSO, which we are developing, is one such proposal. For example, it predicts whether the other hand can support a part after it is moved, then selects the next motion. Selection or reselection among multiple candidates remains unimplemented.

World modelPredict the state after a skill

A model that predicts the state of the robot and the workpiece after a skill is executed. For each candidate skill, it predicts the resulting state and the prospect of reaching the goal. It is not included in the current research implementation (unimplemented).
