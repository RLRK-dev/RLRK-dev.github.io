Our vision

Taking on manual operations that are hard to automate

Flexible objects such as cables change shape with grasping and contact. People assess their condition and adjust force and sequence as they work. A robot needs to check that condition, adapt its motion and connect one operation to the next.

RLR Kashiwanoha focuses on operations that are hard to automate, such as cable routing and installation. Our research addresses the sequence from regrasping and support transfer through routing, fastening, terminal connection, completion checks and recovery.

Case studies

  • High-voltage junction boxAutomotive electrical components | Harness routing, Support transfer, Terminal connection
  • Small control panelIndustrial electrical equipment | Internal wiring, Door wiring, Terminal connection
  • Washing machine harnessHome appliances | Branch separation and routing, Clip fastening, Connector mating

All cases are studies we are conducting on our own initiative.

The future we are working toward

We aim for production lines in which automation can build on earlier development results even when products or operating conditions change. To that end, we work on the following two areas.

  1. 01

    Develop control that responds to the situation

    We research control that combines learning motions from reference actions, correction according to the current state, and connection to the next operation. We will validate individual motions and complete sequences separately.

  2. 02

    Include safety, quality and recovery in the process

    Conditions for proceeding and stopping, human intervention and recovery are part of the process. We clarify the roles of equipment and control, and build the evidence needed before moving to physical hardware.

From simulation toward the factory floor

The work we currently undertake, for the processes agreed for each request, covers building simulations, control development, training where needed and validation by condition. For the equipment used to validate on physical hardware (validation hardware), we also define requirements and the equipment configuration, specify equipment, fixtures, and measurement and control interfaces, and prepare test plans. Specification work can be commissioned on its own, with evidence, assumptions, open issues and the owners responsible for confirming them documented.

We plan to develop Real2Sim, which brings information from physical equipment into models, and Sim2Real, which connects control to that equipment. By defining responsibilities with system integrators and equipment manufacturers, we aim to expand into site deployment and ongoing software support.
